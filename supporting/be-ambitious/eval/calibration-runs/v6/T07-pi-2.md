## Rewritten prompt

```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix. Make it a decision-ready report, not just an incident summary.

Assumption: the team needs a defensible ordering of fix candidates, with uncertainty visible, rather than an apparently precise ranking unsupported by the notes.

Evidence and boundaries:
- Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy.
- First check whether the notes are supplied and establish the reporting period. If the notes are missing, request them before drafting. If the period is unclear, ask for clarification rather than inventing dates.
- Check whether I can arrange review by an already-authorized team member familiar with checkout incident triage who did not author the report. Do not contact anyone or share notes yourself. Without that review, label the report unverified.
- Treat the supplied notes as incomplete observations, not a complete record of checkout traffic or failures. Do not infer failure rates, financial losses, or trends without the necessary evidence.

Build the report from the evidence:
1. Reconstruct distinct incidents using identifiers already present in the notes. Distinguish duplicate descriptions from separate failures; flag ambiguous matches. Cite note identifiers or neutral labels you assign.
2. Group incidents by observed failure mode. Separate symptoms, causes asserted in the notes, and your hypotheses. Test the belief that similar failures share a cause by looking for contradictory details and plausible alternative explanations. Do not turn recurrence into proof of a common cause.
3. Use qualitative failure-mode analysis to distinguish reported customer impact, recurrence, and the strength of causal evidence. Mark unknowns instead of assigning unsupported scores.
4. Use decision analysis to prioritize fix candidates: explain the evidence for each candidate, why it should precede alternatives, and what uncertainty could reverse that ordering. If implementation effort is undocumented, leave it unknown. Do not claim an optimal ranking when the evidence supports only a shortlist.

Deliver one concise weekly report containing:
- Reporting period, evidence coverage, and important limitations.
- An executive recommendation: the leading fix candidate, or an explicit explanation of why no single candidate is yet defensible.
- A compact table of recurring failure patterns, distinct documented incident counts or count uncertainty, reported impact, causal confidence, supporting notes, and recommended priority.
- For each leading candidate, the proposed corrective outcome—not code changes—and the key uncertainty that could change the decision.
- A short “What would change this recommendation?” section identifying evidence gaps without seeking new access or collecting additional data.

Recommendations are not authorization to act. Keep this deliverable to the report.
With my approval only: undertake follow-on work beyond this report under a separate proposal defining its scope and permitted evidence. The prohibited actions above remain excluded.

Judge: an already-authorized checkout-triage reviewer, independent of the report’s author, checks the report against the supplied notes. Pass only if material factual claims are traceable, incident counts disclose duplication uncertainty, symptoms and causal hypotheses remain distinct, priorities follow from documented evidence, and decision-changing unknowns are explicit. Revise any failed items using only the supplied notes. Until this review passes, leave the report unverified.
```
