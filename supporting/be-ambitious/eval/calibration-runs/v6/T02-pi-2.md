## Rewritten prompt

```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. Leaders must decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. The deck is required, must be at most 12 slides, and is the sole deliverable; put supporting calculations, limitations, and checks in its speaker notes rather than a companion report. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers.

Assumption: The goal is to direct the next investment toward durable, economically valuable growth—not merely toward the channel with the largest historical revenue.
Assumption: A useful decision includes the recommended priority, the evidence supporting it, and the conditions that would reverse it.

First check dependencies: the raw customer and revenue exports have not yet been shared. Ask me to provide them through an existing approved channel. Confirm their coverage, definitions, customer identifiers, transaction dates, and treatment of refunds, cancellations, and reactivations. Check which existing analysis and presentation tools are available. Ask whether an investment envelope and relevant internal cost or margin data already exist. Do not assume access, purchase tools, or invent missing inputs. If inputs remain unavailable, prepare the required deck with explicit evidence gaps and a conditional decision framework rather than fabricated findings.

Make this a decision deck, not a performance recap:
- Open with the retention-versus-acquisition decision, your recommendation or conditional recommendation, and the strongest evidence and uncertainty.
- Distinguish historical performance from the expected return on the next investment. Show what available evidence can—and cannot—establish about incremental returns.
- Present the assumptions, break-even conditions, and risks that could change the preferred investment. If no option is robustly supported, identify the specific missing evidence preventing a defensible choice.

Start from the raw exports, not just summary metrics. Using existing tools, create reproducible calculations in the working materials:
- Reconcile revenue to the supplied source totals, check duplicate records and missing identifiers, and document metric definitions.
- Use cohort analysis to compare customers at equivalent tenure and observation windows; distinguish customer retention from retained revenue, and separate acquisition growth from repeat-customer revenue without double counting.
- Where the data support it, use a revenue bridge to show which customer movements explain the quarter. Do not label observed differences as causal effects.

Test the premise that the apparent growth opportunity is also the better investment. Use contribution economics only where costs and margins are supported; otherwise label results as revenue-based, not profit or ROI estimates. Examine whether cohort mix, customer concentration, or incomplete observation windows explain the apparent winner. If a recommendation emerges from historical cohorts, check it against a comparable cohort or period not used to select it, where sufficient data exist; otherwise leave that confirmation unverified.

Borrow decision analysis: show break-even values for the uncertain inputs most likely to reverse the recommendation, using evidence-backed ranges where available and clearly labeled scenarios otherwise. Red-team the recommendation by stating the strongest credible case for the alternative and testing whether that case changes the decision.

With my approval only: ask an existing finance or revenue-operations colleague who did not author the analysis to independently review the deck; use no external reviewer or new data sharing.

Judge: First confirm access to the existing validation tools and, if approved, the independent reviewer. Mechanical checks pass when the deck has at most 12 slides, source totals reconcile within a stated rounding tolerance, cohort calculations are reproducible, and material claims are traceable to supplied data or labeled assumptions. The independent reviewer passes the decision quality only if the recommendation follows from the evidence, cohort comparisons are fair, uncertainty and reversal conditions are explicit, and historical associations are not presented as incremental causal returns. Record check results and any review verdict in speaker notes. Missing evidence or an unavailable reviewer leaves the affected claims or review unverified; do not imply validation that has not occurred.
```
