Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix. Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide.

You may not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy. Do not seek other data sources or reconstruct redacted information.

Assumption: the report should help the team choose defensible fix candidates, not merely summarize incidents; choosing or implementing a fix remains the team’s responsibility.

First check whether the notes and reporting period are available. If the notes are missing, ask me to provide them. If the period is unclear, ask or explicitly bound the report to the supplied notes without inventing dates. Check whether an owner-arranged independent reviewer is available, but do not contact anyone or share materials. Missing reviewer access must not block drafting.

Produce one weekly report, sized for a team prioritization discussion:
- State the reporting window, source coverage, and important gaps.
- Group the reported failures into candidate recurring patterns. Cite each supporting note using its supplied identifier, or assign stable local labels if none exist.
- Distinguish separate incidents from duplicate descriptions. Explain grouping decisions and flag cases that could belong to different patterns.
- For each pattern, summarize the observed checkout symptom, documented customer or operational impact, recurrence evidence, and any explicit trend supported by comparable notes. Distinguish symptoms, suspected causes, and confirmed causes.
- Recommend a prioritized shortlist of fix candidates. For each, state why it deserves attention, what the notes actually support, and what uncertainty could change its priority. Do not invent implementation effort, revenue loss, affected-user counts, or failure rates. Note counts are not checkout failure rates.
- End with the decisions the team can make from this evidence and the questions the notes cannot answer.

Use these methods to deepen the report:
1. Evidence matrix: map each consequential claim and recommendation back to the original redacted notes, rather than relying on a narrative summary. Keep traceability inside the report.
2. Competing-explanation test: challenge the belief that repeated symptoms share a cause. Look for contradictory details and alternative groupings; label unresolved causes as hypotheses.
3. Decision analysis and sensitivity testing: prioritize using documented impact and recurrence, with evidence confidence shown separately. Check whether plausible alternative groupings or uncertain impact would change the top recommendation. If the ordering is unstable, present a tied tier or conditional recommendation rather than false precision.

Do not claim that a recommended fix will resolve the failures unless the notes establish that connection. Missing evidence is not evidence of absence.

If you recommend activity beyond producing this report, place it in a separate proposal, not among authorized next steps. Each proposal line must begin “With my approval only:”. Do not propose any prohibited activity, even conditionally.

With my approval only: arrange an independent review by a checkout-reliability or incident-analysis reviewer using only these same redacted notes; no outreach, delegation, or data sharing is authorized by this prompt.

Judge: the independent reviewer checks source traceability, duplicate handling, symptom-versus-cause distinctions, uncertainty, and priority robustness against the supplied notes. Pass only if every consequential factual claim is supported, every recommendation has an explicit evidence-based rationale, and no unsupported cause, rate, impact, or stable ranking is asserted. Until that review runs and passes, label the report “Draft — independent validation pending.”
