```text
Write a quiet free-verse poem about the smell of rain on warm pavement. It is for its own sake, not for marketing or publication.

Assumption: the aim is a poem that lets a reader smell that moment again, so exact sensory detail matters more than statements about rain.

Before drafting, privately list the raw particulars as a person would actually notice them: the air in the seconds before the first drops, the first sharp hit when they land on hot concrete, the dusty, stony smell that follows, how it thins as the pavement cools. Add the sounds, temperature, and light around it. Treat the smell the way a perfumer describes a scent: an opening, a middle, and a fade, changing over a minute or two. Let that change give the poem its shape.

Write three short drafts with different approaches, for example one held in a single instant, one following the smell from start to fade, and one centered on someone noticing it. Pick the strongest. Revise it by reading it aloud line by line. Cut any word that is there for ornament, sound alone, or explanation. Replace stock rain images and abstract words with things you could point at. Keep the tone low and unhurried.

Requirements:
- Plain language. Prefer everyday words to technical or literary ones.
- Free verse with no rhyme scheme. Check line endings for accidental end-rhymes and change them.
- Under 120 words.
- Output the poem only. No explanatory note, drafts, or commentary after the poem.

Judge (run before output; add nothing to the output): count the words; the count must be under 120. Then check the poem against this rubric as a working poet would: (1) every word is plain and everyday; (2) no rhyme scheme and no clustered end-rhymes; (3) the tone is quiet throughout; (4) at least three concrete details are specific to rain on warm pavement rather than rain in general; (5) no stock phrases. Pass: under 120 words and all five items pass. Revise and recheck until it passes. This self-check guides revision only. Independent confirmation requires a separate reader who gets only the poem and this rubric, and the poem stays unverified until that reader passes it.
```
