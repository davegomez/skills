```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: “Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused.”

Give exactly one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Assumption: The revised sentence should be usable as direct feedback to the colleague.

Work from the supplied wording alone:
- Use an evidence audit to distinguish the criticism that must remain—difficulty following the presentation—from claims about other people’s reactions that you cannot independently establish. Add no examples, causes, or meeting details.
- Use face-saving communication: direct the criticism at the presentation’s comprehensibility, not the colleague’s competence or character. Do not soften it into praise or an ambiguous hint.
- Test the premise that candor needs harshness: retain the negative assessment while removing contemptuous phrasing and unnecessary claims to speak for everyone.
- In the short explanation, identify why the original wording risks humiliation and why the revision remains candid.

First check whether an already-authorized independent reviewer is available; do not obtain new access or delegate this task. If none is available, leave independent validation unverified. Keep the final output exclusively to the revised sentence and short explanation.

Judge: An independent reviewer experienced in workplace communication evaluates the output against five criteria: exactly one revised sentence plus a short explanation; unmistakable preservation of “difficult to follow”; criticism of the presentation rather than the person; no invented events, diagnoses, or performance-management advice; and an explanation that accounts for both candor and humiliation risk. Pass only if all five criteria are satisfied. Without that review, do not claim a verified pass.
```
