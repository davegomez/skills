```text
Tell me why customers churned last month, distinguishing what the available evidence establishes from what remains unknown.

Constraints: I have no raw event logs, CRM export, or database access to share right now. The only supplied information is that the sales team thinks onboarding is the cause. Do not buy data, scrape customer information, or request production credentials. Do not treat sales’ belief as an observed customer-level finding.

Assumption: The explanation should help me decide whether onboarding deserves further investigation, rather than merely repeat the sales team’s diagnosis.

First establish the evidence boundary:
- Check whether any relevant, already-authorized aggregate summaries or documented customer feedback have already been supplied. Do not assume they exist or require me to provide unavailable sources.
- Identify missing definitions that would materially change the explanation, especially what counts as churn and which customer population is included. Keep “last month” as written unless its dates are established.
- Check whether an already-authorized independent reviewer is available for the judge below. Missing evidence or reviewer access must remain explicit; neither prevents a bounded answer.

Use these methods:
1. Causal inference: distinguish a possible cause from a symptom or correlated characteristic. Explain what evidence would connect onboarding problems to subsequent churn, and what could produce the same apparent pattern without onboarding being the cause.
2. Differential diagnosis: compare the onboarding hypothesis with a small set of plausible alternatives. Label every alternative as a hypothesis, not a finding. For each, identify an observation that would distinguish it from onboarding.
3. Red-team falsification: state what would weaken or overturn the onboarding explanation. Do not interpret the absence of evidence as evidence against it.

Test the task’s underlying belief: does the supplied information justify attributing churn to onboarding? With only the sales team’s opinion, it does not. Explain that limit directly without implying onboarding has been ruled out.

Deliver a concise answer containing:
- What we can responsibly say about why customers churned last month.
- What we cannot yet establish, including whether onboarding caused churn or how much churn it explains.
- A compact hypothesis comparison, separating supplied information from proposed explanations.
- The smallest prioritized evidence check that would most change confidence in onboarding, explaining which competing predictions it would distinguish. Describe the check; do not initiate data collection, customer outreach, or new access.
- The decision this evidence currently supports—and any decision, such as committing to an onboarding overhaul, that it does not yet support.

With my approval only: arrange an independent retention analyst’s review of the noncustomer-specific answer and reasoning; do not share customer information or obtain new system access.

Judge: an independent retention analyst reviews the answer for evidence provenance, causal validity, meaningful competing explanations, a discriminating next check, and compliance with every constraint. Pass only if all five criteria are satisfied and no causal attribution exceeds the available evidence. This review judges the answer’s reasoning, not the factual cause of churn. Until the review runs, label independent validation unverified; until supporting evidence exists, leave the cause of churn unverified.
```
