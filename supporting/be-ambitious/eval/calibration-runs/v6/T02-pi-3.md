```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck is required, must be at most 12 slides total, and must help leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers. Deliver only the deck; include material findings, limitations, and verification status within it.

Assumption: leaders need a defensible investment recommendation, not merely a retrospective comparison of retention and acquisition metrics.

First check dependencies:
- The raw customer and revenue exports have been offered but not shared. Request them and confirm their coverage, fields, customer identifiers, date definitions, and reconciliation totals. Do not invent findings before receiving evidence.
- Check which existing tools and already-authorized internal records are available for analysis and validation. Determine whether acquisition spend, retention costs, contribution margins, and investment constraints are available; do not assume the exports contain them.
- Identify missing inputs early. Continue with supported analysis, but label any investment comparison that depends on missing inputs as conditional or unverified. Missing data must not prevent delivery of the required deck.

Build toward the decision:
- State the recommendation, evidence strength, principal uncertainty, and precise decision requested. If the evidence cannot distinguish the options, say so and identify the decision threshold rather than manufacture a winner.
- Compare retention and acquisition over the same explicit horizon and on comparable economic terms. Where supported, use incremental contribution after relevant costs; otherwise show clearly labeled revenue proxies and break-even conditions. Do not equate historical revenue with the return on additional investment.
- Describe what would have to be true for the recommendation to reverse. Keep any proposed investment conditional on leadership’s decision; do not implement it.

Work from the raw evidence:
- Validate customer identifiers, duplicates, missing values, transaction timing, refunds, and currency consistency where relevant. Reconcile reported revenue to the supplied export totals, explaining any exclusions or discrepancies.
- Define retention, churn, reactivation, and acquisition explicitly. Use cohort analysis to distinguish customer mix and tenure effects from underlying performance, comparing cohorts at equivalent maturity and accounting for incomplete observation.
- Use a revenue bridge to explain changes through retained, expanded, contracted, churned, new, and reactivated customer revenue where the data supports those distinctions. Avoid double counting.
- Test the premise that historical retention or acquisition performance identifies the better next investment. Separate observed results from assumptions about incremental effects; observational differences alone do not establish causation.

Borrow methods that expose decision risk:
- From capital budgeting, use break-even analysis to show the cost or incremental-response threshold at which each option becomes preferable.
- From decision analysis, use bounded sensitivity scenarios grounded in available evidence. Identify the assumption that most changes the recommendation; do not present unsupported probabilities or precision.
- From adversarial review, construct the strongest evidence-based case against the preferred option and test whether plausible changes in margins, costs, timing, or response reverse it.

Keep the deck decision-sized. Prioritize the decision, recommendation, reconciled business performance, comparable cohort evidence, economics, reversal thresholds, and limitations. Cite source periods and definitions on relevant slides. Any appendix or verification slide counts toward the 12-slide maximum. Keep working analyses internal; do not add a companion report.

With my approval only: ask an existing finance or analytics colleague who did not author the analysis to independently review the recommendation and supporting evidence using the rubric below.

Judge: use available existing tools to check the slide count, arithmetic, revenue reconciliation, and consistency of definitions and comparison periods. Mechanical checks pass only when the deck has at most 12 slides and all reported figures reconcile within explicitly stated rounding, with exclusions explained. If the independent review is approved and available, the reviewer must find no unresolved material issue in source traceability, cohort comparability, treatment of incremental economics, uncertainty, or whether the recommendation follows from the evidence. Show check results and material limitations within the deck. Until checks run, mark affected results unverified; without the independent review, do not claim the recommendation is independently validated.
```
