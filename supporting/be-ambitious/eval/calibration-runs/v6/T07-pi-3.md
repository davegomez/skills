```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix.

You may inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. You may not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy. Do not seek additional sources or access. If you think we should move beyond the report, make that a separate proposal needing my approval; do not execute it. Prohibited activities remain prohibited.

Assumption: The report should help the team choose the next fix to investigate, not merely summarize incidents; the notes may not establish a root cause or a definitive fix.

First checks:
- Confirm that the redacted notes are available and identify the reporting week from them. If either is missing or ambiguous, ask me rather than inventing it.
- Check whether an owner-designated independent reviewer is available to evaluate the report using the same notes. Do not contact or delegate to anyone. If reviewer availability is unconfirmed, leave the review unverified.
- Identify which decision-relevant evidence the notes actually contain: incident timestamps, affected checkout steps, observed symptoms, impact, recovery actions, and evidence about causes. Treat missing information as unknown.

Build the report from traceable evidence:
- Use existing note identifiers, or assign neutral reference labels, so readers can trace each material claim to the supplied notes without exposing additional sensitive details.
- Apply incident deduplication and failure-signature grouping: distinguish multiple notes about one incident from separate incidents, and group recurrence by observed checkout behavior rather than assuming a shared cause.
- Distinguish documented observations, hypotheses, and unknowns. Do not treat a recovery action as proof of cause.
- Reconcile reported counts with the underlying notes. Call them “documented incidents,” not total failures. Do not infer failure rates, trends, revenue loss, or total customer impact without the necessary evidence and comparable coverage.
- Test the report’s central premise: do the notes demonstrate recurring failures, or only superficially similar symptoms? Preserve that distinction in the recommendations.

Make the weekly report decision-ready:
1. Open with the strongest supported recommendation about what to investigate or fix next, its evidence, and the principal uncertainty. If the evidence cannot support a single choice, say so and present the decision-relevant alternatives.
2. Include a compact comparison table: failure pattern, documented recurrence, documented impact, supporting note references, confidence in the grouping or cause, and recommended next decision.
3. Use qualitative multi-criteria decision analysis to compare recurrence, documented severity, and strength of causal evidence. Explain trade-offs; do not manufacture numerical scores, effort estimates, or precision unsupported by the notes.
4. Apply a sensitivity check: would plausible deduplication or alternative grouping change the top priority? Show any consequential instability.
5. Red-team the leading recommendation by looking within the supplied notes for contradictory cases, alternative explanations, and evidence that would favor a different priority.
6. Close with the uncertainties that could change the decision. Keep recommendations distinct from actions taken.

Keep the work within the report. Do not create a recurring workflow or a separate evidence deliverable; include necessary traceability and limitations in the report itself.
With my approval only: undertake any specifically proposed work beyond the report, described in a separate proposal with its scope, dependencies, and risks. This does not authorize any prohibited activity.

Judge: An owner-designated reviewer other than the author evaluates the report against the supplied redacted notes. Pass only if every material factual claim is traceable, incident counts reconcile, recurrence is distinguished from assumed common cause, unsupported metrics are absent, and the reviewer judges that the recommendation and its uncertainty are clear enough to support a next-fix decision—or that abstaining from a ranking is justified. Until that review occurs, mark the report’s validation as unverified.
```
