```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."
Give one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Goal: a sentence the colleague hears as honest information about the work rather than a verdict on them, and can use to make the next presentation easier to follow.
Assumption: the manager will say or send this sentence to the colleague directly and privately.
Assumption: the manager is willing to offer informal help, such as talking it through. If not, drop the forward-looking clause described below.

Method:
- Ground the critique in named criteria, and say what each one finds in the original:
  - Brown and Levinson's face-threat analysis: which words attack the colleague's competence or public standing. "A mess" is a global judgment of the work. "Everyone" adds public exposure.
  - Nonviolent Communication's observation-versus-evaluation distinction: which parts are labels and which are reportable experience.
  - The impact step of the Situation-Behavior-Impact model: state the effect on the audience instead of a label for the work.
- Keep every claim within what the original sentence supplies. "Everyone was confused" asserts other people's inner states, which the manager may not be able to vouch for. Prefer wording the manager can stand behind. Say in the explanation which wording you chose and why.
- Add no specifics about slides, sections, timing, questions asked, or anyone's reactions.
- Keep the candor. The revision must plainly say the presentation was hard to follow. Fail any draft that buries or blurs the point with hedges ("maybe a little"), padding compliments, or a question that never states the problem. This is the "ruinous empathy" failure mode from Radical Candor.
- Where it needs no invented facts, end the sentence pointing forward with an informal offer, such as going through it together. Propose no formal plan, review, or process.
- Red-team the draft twice before finalizing:
  - Read it as the colleague would on a bad day. Does any word sting beyond the core message, or sound sarcastic or patronizing?
  - Read it as a blunt observer. Did the softening erase the message?
  - Revise until both readings pass.

Deliverable: one revised sentence and a short explanation that carries the critique of the original. Nothing else.

Judge: a reviewer other than the author (the manager, or a peer the manager trusts) checks:
(1) the output is exactly one revised sentence plus a short explanation that critiques the original;
(2) the sentence unambiguously says the presentation was difficult to follow;
(3) it addresses the work and its effect, not the colleague's character or ability;
(4) it adds no facts about the meeting beyond the original sentence;
(5) it contains no diagnosis of the colleague;
(6) it suggests no performance-management process;
(7) both red-team readings pass.
Pass only if every criterion is met.
```

- `altitude`: The original outcome is a less harsh sentence. The improved outcome is a sentence the colleague can act on, ending in an optional informal offer of help. It stays one sentence, with no process and no invented facts. Small risk: the offer commits the manager to something, so it sits behind an assumption line.
- `depth`: Named criteria (face-threat analysis, observation vs. evaluation, SBI's impact step) replace a gut-feel critique. The prompt also questions the unverifiable "everyone was confused" claim instead of carrying it forward. This makes the critique more specific and the sentence more defensible. Scope and cost are unchanged.
- `range`: Two methods come from outside management writing. Pragmatics/linguistics supplies the face-threat analysis, and a two-sided red-team catches both failure modes: too harsh and too soft. Together they find stings and over-softening that a single reading misses.
- Skipped: none. All three lenses apply.
- Owner actions:
  - Confirm the delivery is direct and private.
  - Confirm you're willing to offer informal help, or ask for that clause to be dropped.
  - Name the reviewer who will act as judge.
