```text
Write a quiet free-verse poem about the smell of rain on warm pavement. It is for its own sake, not for marketing or publication.

Fixed requirements: plain language; under 120 words; free verse with no rhyme scheme; deliver only the poem, with no explanatory note after it.

Assumption: "quiet" means restrained in tone: small in scale, no spectacle, no exclamations. The poem should feel like one real moment rather than a statement about rain.

Method:
1. Start from raw sensory material, not the stock image. Before drafting, privately list 15 to 20 concrete things someone could notice in that moment. Examples: heat held in the pavement, the first dark spots of rain, steam lifting, dust smell rising, where the speaker stands, what the sound does, how the air changes on the skin, the time of day. Leave out the science words (petrichor, geosmin, ozone), because they break plain language.
2. Follow the smell through time, the way a perfumer describes a scent by how it opens, holds, and fades. There is the sharp first minute when drops hit hot ground, the fuller middle, and the thin cool trace after. Let that arc, or one part of it, shape the poem instead of a description of rain in general.
3. Cut stock moves: "petrichor," "the earth sighs" or "the earth breathes," tears, rain as cleansing, and any ending that explains what the smell means. Look for one exact detail that few rain poems would contain, and keep it only if it is true to the moment.
4. Draft long, then cut to under 120 words. Sound the poem out line by line and break lines where the breath pauses, so the line breaks carry the quiet without rhyme.
5. Before delivering, check privately: the actual word count is under 120; there are no end-rhymes and no rhyme pattern; every word is plain; nothing follows the poem.

Judge: I, the owner, read the poem cold. It passes only if every criterion holds:
- it is under 120 words by count;
- it has no rhyme scheme and nothing after the poem;
- I can name at least one specific image I haven't met in a rain poem before;
- no line explains the feeling;
- it reads quietly aloud.
Until I read it, the qualitative criteria stay unverified.
```

- `depth`: The poem starts from listed concrete particulars and cuts stock rain imagery. This moves it from a generic rain poem toward one observed moment. There is no scope or cost effect.
- `depth`: The private pre-delivery check covers word count, rhyme, plainness, and the absence of a note. It enforces the fixed requirements without adding anything to the output.
- `depth`: The judge is the owner, with a named pass condition, including "one image I haven't met in a rain poem before." This turns "quiet" and "plain" into checkable criteria.
- `range`: Perfumery's practice of describing a scent as it opens, holds, and fades gives the poem a shape that follows the smell over time instead of a static description. Sounding out the line breaks is a poet's tool for carrying quiet without rhyme. Neither adds scope.
- Skipped: `altitude`. The owner wants the poem for its own sake, so there is no downstream decision or outcome to climb toward.
- Owner actions: read the poem and apply the judge's criteria. No approvals are needed, because nothing was added beyond the original scope.
