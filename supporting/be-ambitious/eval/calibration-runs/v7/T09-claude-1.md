```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."
Give one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Assumption: the manager will say this privately to the colleague, and the real goal is that the colleague accepts the point and knows the next presentation needs to be easier to follow.
Assumption: candor and care are not a trade-off here. The revision must be as clear as the original, not softened into vagueness.

Method:
- Work phrase by phrase from the original words, not a general impression. For each phrase, name what it does to the listener:
  - "a mess" is a global, evaluative label on the work.
  - "everyone was confused" speaks for absent people and puts the colleague's failure in front of an audience.
  - "Your" points at the person rather than the work.
- Apply observation-versus-evaluation, from nonviolent communication: describe what the manager perceived, such as "hard to follow", instead of a verdict such as "a mess".
- Apply face-threat analysis, from politeness theory: remove what makes the criticism public or total, and keep the criticism itself.
- Speak from the manager's own perception, using "I" or "it seemed".
  - Do not claim any fact the original does not contain.
  - If you narrow "everyone" to the manager's view, phrase it as the manager's read of the room, not a new claim about specific people.
- The sentence may close by inviting joint work on the next one. That makes it actionable without adding a process.
- Red-team the draft twice before finalizing:
  - Read it as the colleague on a bad day. Flag any word that still stings, sounds passive-aggressive, or sounds sarcastic.
  - Read it as a skeptic. Flag hedges that would let the colleague hear "it was fine."
  - Revise until neither reading finds a problem.

Output: the critique folded into a short explanation, plus exactly one revised sentence. Nothing else.

Judge: the manager, or a trusted peer other than the author, checks:
(1) it is exactly one sentence;
(2) it still says plainly that the presentation was difficult to follow;
(3) it contains no meeting details absent from the original;
(4) it does not diagnose the colleague's skill, effort, or state;
(5) it mentions no performance-management process;
(6) it criticizes the presentation, not the person, and does not invoke an audience of witnesses;
(7) neither red-team reading finds a sting or a dodge;
(8) the explanation is short and names why each original phrase was changed.
Pass only if every criterion is met.
```
