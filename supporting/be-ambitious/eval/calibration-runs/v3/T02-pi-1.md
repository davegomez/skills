Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck is required and must be at most 12 slides. Its purpose is to help leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers.

Assumption to confirm: leaders want the investment with the stronger incremental economic return, subject to cash, capacity, and risk constraints—not simply the stronger historical growth metric.

First check dependencies:
- Request the raw customer and revenue exports; they have been offered but not shared. Confirm their coverage, definitions, identifiers, and permitted use.
- Confirm the quarter being reviewed, the decision’s investment horizon, and any budget or capacity constraints. If these remain unspecified, label working assumptions and show where they affect the recommendation.
- Check whether existing, authorized sources contain acquisition spend, retention-program costs, gross margins, refunds, and relevant intervention history. Check which existing tools can analyze the exports reproducibly. Missing inputs are limitations, not permission to obtain new access or buy tools.
- Confirm whether an authorized, qualified internal reviewer is available. Do not assume access or assign work.

Build a decision case, not just a performance recap:
1. Establish a trustworthy baseline from the raw exports. Reconcile customer and revenue totals to available existing business records; check duplicates, missing identifiers, date boundaries, and definitions of new, retained, reactivated, and churned customers. Document unresolved discrepancies.
2. Use cohort analysis and, where observation windows differ, survival analysis to distinguish retention changes from customer mix and incomplete follow-up. Separate customer retention from revenue retention, and distinguish recurring revenue from one-off purchases where the data supports it.
3. Compare retention and acquisition on the same investment amount and time horizon. Estimate incremental contribution after relevant costs, payback, and uncertainty where inputs support them. Do not compare retention revenue with acquisition profit or present historical averages as marginal returns.
4. Test the belief underlying the choice: do the available records support a claim that additional spending on either option will produce additional value? Use existing intervention evidence where available. Apply causal-inference checks for selection bias, confounding, and reverse causation; otherwise label estimates as scenarios rather than measured causal effects.
5. Use decision analysis to identify switching thresholds: the acquisition cost, retention uplift, margin, or payback assumption at which the preferred choice changes. Show which uncertainties matter enough to reverse the decision. Recommend one option when supported; if neither is justified, state precisely what cannot yet be concluded.
6. Red-team the recommendation with the strongest plausible case for the alternative. Test whether the recommendation survives adverse but evidence-grounded assumptions. If forecasts are used and sufficient history exists, reserve an untouched historical period for a backtest; evidence used to revise a model is no longer held-out validation.

Keep the deck within 12 slides, including any appendix slides. Lead with the decision requested, recommendation, confidence, and conditions that would reverse it. Include only the business context, reconciled performance evidence, comparable economics, uncertainty, and constraints needed to support that decision. Use source references and speaker notes for definitions, assumptions, calculation traceability, and validation status. Do not add slides to accommodate validation.

With my approval only: ask a qualified internal reviewer, using existing staff and an existing review opportunity, to independently challenge the evidence and recommendation against the rubric below. Do not create a new meeting or recurring workflow.

Judge and observable pass condition:
- Using confirmed existing tools, run reproducible checks of source reconciliation, cohort assignment, key calculations, and consistent comparison horizons. Pass when displayed figures trace to the supplied data, arithmetic checks pass, and any unresolved discrepancies are quantified and their decision impact is disclosed.
- If a forecast is used, report its held-out error against a simple historical baseline; if no valid holdout exists, mark predictive performance unverified.
- The independent reviewer checks whether the deck makes the choice explicit, compares like-for-like economics, distinguishes causal evidence from scenarios, exposes decision-reversing assumptions, and supports the recommendation without unsupported claims. Qualitative validation passes only when that reviewer confirms these criteria; otherwise mark it pending or unverified.
- Report validation status within the deck or its notes. The required deliverable remains a deck of at most 12 slides, ready for the leadership meeting in two weeks.
