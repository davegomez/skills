Prepare the required quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck must be at most 12 slides and enable leaders to decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers. Preparing the recommendation does not authorize spending or implementation.

Assumption to confirm: leaders want to allocate investment toward sustainable incremental economic value, not simply maximize reported revenue or customer counts.

First check dependencies:
- The raw customer and revenue exports are available to be provided but have not been shared. Request them before making company-specific findings. Confirm their coverage, definitions, identifiers, and permitted use.
- Confirm the decision horizon, investment amount or resource constraint, and success metric. Ask whether acquisition costs, retention costs, margins, and historical intervention records are available through already-authorized sources. Do not assume revenue exports contain these inputs.
- Confirm which existing analysis tools are available. Use only authorized tools and data; identify missing inputs explicitly. If evidence remains unavailable, still prepare the required deck, clearly distinguishing supported findings, modeled scenarios, and unresolved questions.

Build a decision-ready investment case, not just a retrospective:
1. Start with the decision leaders must make, the supported recommendation or conditional recommendation, and the evidence that would reverse it. Compare retention and acquisition over the same horizon and resource constraint. Use incremental contribution after relevant costs where supported; do not present revenue as profit or historical averages as marginal returns.
2. Work from the raw exports. Use SQL or existing spreadsheet tools to reconcile customer and revenue totals, check duplicate identifiers and missing periods, and construct acquisition cohorts. Define churn, retention, expansion, and reactivation explicitly; distinguish customer retention from revenue retention. Decompose revenue movement into new, retained, expanded, contracted, and lost revenue without double-counting.
3. Test the premise behind the choice: does the evidence identify a retention problem, an acquisition constraint, or merely a change in customer mix? Examine cohort age, tenure, and relevant segments before attributing differences to either investment. Use causal-inference discipline: historical associations do not establish the effect of an untested intervention.
4. Model comparable retention and acquisition scenarios using traceable inputs. Separate observed quantities from assumed intervention effects. Show feasible ranges, break-even intervention effects, and the conditions under which the preferred investment changes. Where costs or causal effects are unavailable, show the threshold needed to justify investment rather than inventing an ROI.
5. Use decision analysis to expose downside risk and the cost of choosing incorrectly. Red-team the leading recommendation by constructing the strongest plausible case for the alternative, checking for survivor bias, attribution errors, and timing differences such as acquisition payback versus retention benefit realization.
6. If forecasts materially support the recommendation, test them on an untouched historical period or cohort, where sufficient data exist. Choose the evaluation metric and acceptable error before examining those results. Evidence used to revise the model is development evidence, not independent confirmation; use another untouched slice or leave the revised forecast unverified.

Keep all slides, including appendices, within the 12-slide limit. Prioritize the decision, reconciled quarterly performance, cohort evidence, comparable investment economics, uncertainty and reversal thresholds, and the leadership decision requested. Include concise sources, assumptions, limitations, and validation status inside the deck. Do not manufacture findings to fill slides or replace the deck with an analysis report.

With my approval only: ask an existing, qualified finance or analytics colleague to independently review the investment logic and evidence using already-authorized access; this must not create new access, external sharing, additional staffing, or a recurring workflow.

Judge and observable pass condition:
- Run checks in existing tools: the deck has at most 12 slides; revenue reconciles to the supplied exports within documented rounding; customer movements balance under explicit definitions; and quantitative decision claims trace to source calculations or labeled assumptions. Any unexplained discrepancy means the affected claim has not passed.
- If a forecast is used, report its untouched-period test result against the preselected error tolerance; unavailable testing or failed performance must be visible and must limit reliance on that forecast.
- If approved and available, the independent reviewer must verify that the alternatives use comparable horizons and constraints, uncertainty is explicit, causal claims do not outrun evidence, and leaders can identify both the recommended choice and its reversal conditions. Record unresolved objections. Until that review actually runs and passes, mark the investment recommendation as not independently verified.
Report validation status within the deck; do not count self-assessment as independent review.
