```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."
Give one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Goal (assumption): the colleague should finish reading the sentence clear that the presentation was hard to follow, able to act on it, and not feeling publicly shamed.

Method:
- Break the original into its separate claims and test each against what the manager can stand behind. "A mess" is a global verdict on the work. "Everyone was confused" speaks for the whole audience, which the manager may not be able to support, and it turns feedback into a report of public failure. Keep the core observation (hard to follow). Drop or narrow every claim that goes beyond it.
- Use feedback practice. Following the impact step of the Situation-Behavior-Impact model, state the effect on the speaker with an "I" statement instead of labeling the work. Name the dimension of the problem (following it) without adding specifics the manager did not give.
- Use politeness theory (Brown and Levinson). The remark threatens the colleague's face, so deliver it on record and unambiguous, with redress that does not dilute it. Avoid hedges, compliment sandwiches, or softeners that make "hard to follow" deniable.
- Optionally close with a forward-looking clause, such as an offer to talk it through, so the colleague has a next step. Include it only if it adds no meeting events, diagnosis, or process.
- Red-team the draft twice before answering:
  - As the colleague: does any word sting beyond the message, or imply others were judging them?
  - As a skeptical peer: is the message still unmistakable, or did softening erase it?
  Revise until both reads pass.

Output: exactly the requested deliverable, which is one revised sentence plus a short explanation that carries the critique. Add no validation notes to it.

With my approval only: give the revised sentence, with no names, to 2–3 independent readers who were not in the meeting. Ask each to state the sentence's message in their own words and to rate how it would feel to receive it (1–5).

Judge: before answering, check the draft against these pass/fail items:
1. Exactly one revised sentence.
2. Every factual claim in it appears in the original or is narrower (no new events, audience counts, or causes).
3. No statement about the colleague's ability, effort, nerves, or character.
4. No mention of performance plans, reviews, HR, or any formal process.
5. A reader would say the presentation was hard to follow.
6. Both red-team reads pass.
Items 1–4 are checks against the text. Items 5–6 are qualitative. Your own reading guides revision but does not validate them, so they stay pending until the approved readers' check. Readers pass it if every reader restates "hard to follow" and none rates it humiliating (1).
```
