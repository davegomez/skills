```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix.

Boundaries
- Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy.
- Do not seek additional logs, telemetry, account details, or external sources. Treat missing evidence as a limitation, not permission to obtain it.
- Any move beyond the report must be a separate proposal needing my approval.

First checks
Confirm that the notes are provided and whether they establish the reporting week. If either is missing, ask me for it; do not invent incidents or a reporting period. Check whether an existing, permitted offline tool is available for reconciling counts and references. Do not install software or obtain new access.

Goal
Assumption: the report should help the team choose the next investigation or fix, not merely summarize incidents. Make that choice easier while distinguishing what the notes establish from what remains uncertain.

Analysis
1. Build an evidence ledger from the notes. Assign stable references and extract dates, checkout stage, observed symptoms, stated customer effects, reported causes, mitigations, and outcomes where present. Preserve uncertainty and distinguish reported explanations from demonstrated causes.
2. Test the premise of recurrence. Separate repeated mentions of one incident from distinct incidents, and repeated symptoms from evidence of a shared failure mechanism. Explain ambiguous groupings and show how alternative groupings affect the conclusions.
3. Use failure-mode analysis to identify where checkout breaks and the consequences actually documented. Do not infer failure rates, revenue loss, affected-user totals, or absence of failures from incomplete notes.
4. Use decision analysis to rank candidate fixes or investigations by documented consequences, distinct-incident recurrence, and strength of causal evidence. Use qualitative tiers rather than invented numerical scores. Mark effort and feasibility unknown unless the notes support them.
5. Stress-test the leading recommendation: identify its strongest plausible alternative explanation and the most consequential missing fact. Check whether a different reasonable grouping or interpretation changes the top priority. If the choice is unstable, recommend a bounded investigation rather than presenting a speculative fix as established.

Report
Include:
- Reporting period and a concise decision summary.
- A prioritized table: failure pattern, supporting note references, distinct incident count or range, documented consequences, causal confidence, recommended next action, and unresolved uncertainty.
- An explanation of why the leading priority outranks the alternatives, including the sensitivity check.
- An evidence appendix with incident groupings, duplicate-handling rules, and source references.
- Limitations and validation status.

Describe recommended actions; do not execute them. Keep any proposal beyond producing this report separate from the findings:
With my approval only: undertake follow-up evidence collection, implement a fix, or trial a different workflow under a separately agreed scope; none is authorized by this report.
With my approval only: obtain an owner-arranged review by a qualified checkout or reliability reviewer using the redacted notes and draft. Do not contact a reviewer or share materials yourself.

Judge and pass condition
If a permitted offline tool is available, run a deterministic reconciliation of the evidence ledger and report: every reference resolves, incident totals match the stated groupings, and duplicate mentions are not counted as separate incidents. Report the actual result; otherwise mark these checks pending.
For decision quality, an owner-arranged qualified reviewer must confirm that the leading recommendation is traceable to the notes, separates symptoms from causes, does not rely on unsupported impact claims, and either survives the stated alternative interpretation or explicitly defers the choice pending investigation. The report passes validation only when reconciliation succeeds and the independent review meets all four criteria. Until then, label the corresponding validation unverified; the author's own assessment is not a substitute.
```
