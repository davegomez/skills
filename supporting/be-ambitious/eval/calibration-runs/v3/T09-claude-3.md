Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."

Goal (assumption: the manager wants the colleague to hear the point, keep their dignity, and be able to act on it next time): a revision the colleague can act on, not one that only stings less.

Work from the original's exact words:
- Critique each phrase by what it does to the listener. "A mess" judges the whole work instead of describing its effect. "Everyone was confused" speaks for the whole audience and frames the talk as a public failure. Use two feedback tools to sort what stays from what goes:
  - The nonviolent-communication split between observation and evaluation.
  - The behavior-impact core of Situation-Behavior-Impact (SBI) feedback.
  Keep the impact (the presentation was difficult to follow). Cut the global label and the unscoped "everyone."
- Use politeness theory (Brown and Levinson's face-threatening acts) to choose wording that protects the colleague's dignity without softening the point away. Speak from the manager's own experience, and address the work rather than the person.

- Red-team the draft twice:

  - Read it as a neutral reader. Is it still unmistakable that the presentation was difficult to follow?
  Revise if either check fails. This is a revision aid, not the judge.

Requirements:
- Deliver one revised sentence and a short explanation, nothing else.
- Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow.

- Do not suggest a performance-management process.
- Do not assume the setting (private or public) or any relationship detail the original does not give.

With my approval only: show the revised sentence, without the original, to 3 people not involved in the meeting, for the judge below.

Judge (runs outside the deliverable; until it runs, the result is unverified): independent readers who have not seen the original read the revised sentence and answer two questions:
(a) In your own words, what is the speaker saying about the presentation?
(b) On a 1–5 scale, how humiliated would you feel hearing this from your manager?
Pass: every reader's answer to (a) says the presentation was hard to follow or unclear, and no reader rates (b) above 2.
