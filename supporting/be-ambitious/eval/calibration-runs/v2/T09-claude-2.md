```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."

Deliverable (unchanged): one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Assumption (inferred, not stated): the sentence opens a feedback conversation, so success means the colleague hears the problem clearly, doesn't feel publicly shamed, and can act on it.

Method:
1. Diagnose the original in feedback-practice terms:
   - Situation-Behavior-Impact separates the work from the person.
   - Owned "I" statements replace an unverifiable "everyone" claim.
   - Evaluative words ("a mess") are distinct from observations ("hard to follow").
   - Use only what the sentence itself states. No meeting details, causes, or traits.
2. Apply politeness theory (Brown & Levinson): name which phrases threaten the colleague's "face," and how to reduce that threat without hedging away the message. Watch for the "mum effect," where softening hides the point.
3. Draft at least 3 candidates internally. Aim for one that is at least as clear about the problem as the original and clearly less humiliating. Don't settle for a softer, vaguer sentence. If a candidate loses candor, try another construction before accepting a trade-off.
4. Red-team the finalist from two readings:
   - A defensive colleague: does anything feel like a verdict on them, or an appeal to the crowd?
   - A colleague who wants to miss the point: could they leave thinking it went fine?
   Revise until both readings fail.

Output: the one revised sentence, then a short explanation (3–5 sentences) of what changed and why. Nothing else.

Judge: score the final sentence against this rubric. Report each item pass/fail:
(a) It still says the presentation was difficult to follow.
(b) It speaks for the manager's own experience, not "everyone."
(c) It addresses the work, not the person.
(d) It contains no invented meeting facts, diagnosis, or process.
(e) It is one sentence.
(f) It survives both red-team readings.
Pass = all six pass. Label this as a self-assessment. It is unverified until an independent reader (e.g., the manager or an HR/coaching peer) rates the original and the revision blind on "clear about the problem" and "humiliating" (1–5). The revision passes that check if it scores ≥ the original on clarity and lower on humiliation.
```
