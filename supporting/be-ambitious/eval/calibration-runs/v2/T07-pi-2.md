### Rewritten prompt

```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix. You may inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. You may not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy. If you think we should move beyond the report, make that a separate proposal needing my approval.

Intended result: a weekly report the team can use to choose a defensible fix priority—not merely a summary of incidents.
Assumption: the team values reducing customer harm and recurrence; do not invent business priorities, impact figures, or decision thresholds.

First verify that the supplied redacted notes are available and identify their reporting window. Ask for missing notes or an ambiguous reporting window. Check whether an owner-arranged independent reviewer is available; do not contact anyone. Missing reviewer access must not prevent drafting, but validation remains unverified until review occurs. Use no outside sources or additional access.

Build the report from the notes:
- Create an evidence table with stable note references, incident dates where given, checkout stage, observed symptom, explicitly documented impact, recurrence evidence, and any stated cause. Distinguish observations, incident authors’ hypotheses, and your interpretations.
- Use incident reconstruction and failure-mode classification to identify recurring patterns. Distinguish separate incidents from repeated updates about one incident; disclose uncertain duplicates and their effect on counts.
- Test the belief that repeated symptoms imply a common cause. Compare plausible alternative groupings and identify evidence that supports or contradicts each. Do not present a shared root cause as established unless the notes establish it.
- Count only what the notes support. Report missing coverage and denominators; do not infer failure rates from incident counts. Compare weeks only if supplied notes cover comparable periods. Clearly distinguish “not documented” from “did not happen.”

Use decision analysis to turn the evidence into a short, ranked shortlist of candidate fix areas. Compare documented recurrence, documented harm, confidence in the diagnosis, and any documented effort or dependencies. Leave unknowns explicit rather than assigning invented scores. Red-team the leading recommendation: identify its strongest competing explanation and whether reasonable changes in grouping or priority assumptions would change the ranking. If the evidence cannot support an ordering, present a conditional shortlist rather than false precision.

Deliver:
1. Reporting window, source coverage, and a concise executive summary.
2. Recurring failure patterns, with traceable evidence and supported counts.
3. Candidate fix priorities, their rationale, confidence, and what could change the decision.
4. Important unknowns and limits on the conclusions.
5. A compact evidence appendix sufficient to reproduce the counts and inspect consequential claims.

Keep recommendations distinct from actions taken. If useful work would go beyond the report or require new access, describe it only in a separate “Proposal—requires owner approval” section, including its benefit, scope, dependencies, and evaluation method. Do not execute it. Approval of a proposal is not authorization to bypass the stated restrictions.

Judge: replay the incident tally against the cited notes and provide the reconciliation, then have an owner-arranged independent reviewer assess source fidelity, duplicate handling, observation-versus-inference labeling, and whether the prioritization follows from the evidence. Pass condition: every consequential factual claim is traceable to supplied notes; counts reconcile under the disclosed rules; uncertainties and alternative explanations are explicit; and the reviewer finds no unsupported factual or causal claims or priorities presented as more certain than the evidence permits. Report observed audit results and unresolved discrepancies. Until independent review runs, label the report “Draft—independent validation pending,” not validated.
```
