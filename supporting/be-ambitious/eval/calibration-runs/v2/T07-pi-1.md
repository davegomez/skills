```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix. Make it decision-ready: distinguish well-supported recurring problems from repeated descriptions, and show which fix candidates the available evidence supports prioritizing.

Scope and authority
- Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. Do not consult other sources or infer access to underlying logs, customer records, or systems.
- Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy.
- Any recommendation to move beyond the report must appear in a separate proposal requiring my approval. Do not execute it. This prompt grants no additional authority.
- Assumption: the team wants defensible prioritization, not simply a ranking by how often a failure is mentioned.

Check dependencies first
Confirm whether the notes and reporting week have been supplied. If notes are missing, request them and stop. If the week or coverage is unclear, ask for clarification; any interim analysis must explicitly state its provisional window and limitations.
Check whether an owner-designated reviewer with checkout or incident-triage expertise is available to evaluate the report. Do not contact anyone. If reviewer availability is unconfirmed, include the review rubric and leave independent validation unverified.

Build the evidence before writing the narrative
Use the supplied notes as the rawest permitted evidence. Assign stable source references and build a compact evidence matrix covering:
- incident date, checkout symptom or stage, and recorded impact;
- whether an entry describes a distinct incident, a duplicate, or an uncertain match;
- any stated cause, supporting evidence, attempted mitigation, and recorded result;
- missing or contradictory information that could change a fix decision.

Keep observations, the notes’ own causal claims, and your hypotheses distinct. Do not reconstruct redacted information. Deduplicate only where the notes support doing so, and show ambiguous cases rather than silently resolving them.

Test the report’s central premise
Check whether “recurring failures” actually means repeated instances of a supported common cause, recurring symptoms with unknown causes, or repeated reporting of the same incident. Group by symptom when causal grouping is not justified.

Use evidence synthesis to compare incidents and preserve source traceability. Use qualitative multi-criteria decision analysis to compare fix candidates by documented impact, recurrence evidence, causal confidence, and actionability. Do not invent missing effort estimates, transaction denominators, failure rates, or numerical precision.

Use sensitivity analysis to test whether the suggested priority changes under plausible alternative deduplication or grouping decisions. Red-team the leading recommendation: identify the strongest contradictory note or alternative explanation, and explain whether it weakens the recommendation. Do not present speculative explanations as findings.

Write the weekly report
Include:
1. An executive summary: what recurred, the best-supported fix priorities, and the most consequential uncertainty.
2. A compact comparison table: failure cluster, observed incident count or supported range, documented impact, evidence confidence, source references, and suggested next decision.
3. A short rationale for the leading priorities, including why plausible alternatives rank lower—or why the evidence does not justify a stable ranking.
4. Evidence gaps and limitations, including whether week-over-week comparisons are supported. Absence from the notes is not evidence that a failure did not occur.
5. A concise evidence appendix sufficient to audit the consequential claims.

Recommend what the team should consider fixing; do not implement fixes or imply that proposed fixes have been validated. If useful, add a separately labeled “Proposal requiring owner approval” for work beyond the report, stating its purpose, dependencies, risks, and requested approval. Keep it separate from the report’s findings.

Judge and observable pass condition
Audit every consequential factual claim and prioritization rationale against the supplied notes, recording supporting references, contradictions, and unresolved gaps. Report counts only when supported by the documented incident-identification rules.

Provide an owner-run review rubric for the designated reviewer: source fidelity, defensible recurrence grouping, justified prioritization, explicit uncertainty, and compliance with every scope restriction. The report passes when the source audit finds no unsupported consequential factual claims, each proposed priority is traceable to evidence and survives the stated sensitivity checks—or is clearly labeled unstable—and the reviewer confirms that the report supports choosing what to fix or clearly establishes why the evidence cannot yet support that choice. Document the review findings; until that review occurs, mark independent validation unverified.
```
