### Rewritten prompt

```text
Prepare the required quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck must be at most 12 slides and help leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition.

Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers. Do not purchase tools, incur new spending, or implement an investment decision. Added analysis outside this brief requires owner approval; this prompt grants no additional authority.

Intended result: give leadership an evidence-backed investment recommendation, with the tradeoffs and conditions that would reverse it—not merely a retrospective performance summary.

Assumptions to confirm:
- Leaders want to compare the next increment of investment, not just total historical revenue from retained versus newly acquired customers.
- Financial return, timing, and execution capacity matter to this decision. Confirm the decision criteria rather than silently choosing them.

First check the dependencies:
- The owner can provide raw customer and revenue exports, but has not shared them yet. Request them through an approved channel and confirm permitted use of customer-level data.
- Confirm the meeting date, reporting quarter, available existing tools, and an existing finance or analytics reviewer.
- Inspect the exports before assuming they contain acquisition dates, customer identifiers, revenue adjustments, acquisition sources, costs, or retention interventions. Request only decision-critical missing inputs available within the existing constraints.
- If necessary inputs or permissions are unavailable, identify the limitation and continue with supported analysis. Still prepare the required deck; mark unsupported conclusions and the investment recommendation as provisional.

Build the decision from raw evidence:
1. Reconcile customer and revenue totals to an available authoritative financial source. Document definitions, exclusions, missingness, duplicate handling, and reconciliation differences.
2. Analyze customer cohorts, retention/churn, expansion or contraction, and acquisition performance where the data supports them. Separate cohort maturity, customer mix, and seasonality from apparent changes in performance. Do not substitute revenue retention for profit or treat customer-level records as independent when they are not.
3. Test the premise that historical retention and acquisition performance identifies where the next investment should go. Distinguish descriptive patterns, predictive evidence, and causal evidence. High churn alone does not establish that a retention intervention will work; historical acquisition averages do not establish marginal acquisition returns.
4. Compare feasible retention and acquisition investment scenarios on a common horizon and resource basis. Use incremental contribution, payback, and uncertainty where costs and margins are available. Otherwise show explicit assumptions and break-even thresholds rather than inventing precise ROI.
5. Where sufficient historical periods exist, develop projections on earlier cohorts and test them on later, unused cohorts. Compare against a simple baseline and report measured error. Do not present an untested projection as validated.

Use decision science to make the choice actionable:
- Show which assumptions reverse the recommendation through sensitivity and break-even analysis.
- Account for staff capacity and opportunity cost without proposing a new operating cadence.
- Red-team the preferred option: test whether cohort mix, attribution, missing costs, selection effects, or unrealistic intervention effectiveness could explain its apparent advantage.
- Identify the smallest missing fact that could change the decision. Recommend obtaining it only through permitted existing sources; do not launch experiments or contact customers.

Produce one deck of no more than 12 slides. Prioritize the recommendation and requested decision, business context, reconciled performance, cohort evidence, comparable investment scenarios, uncertainty, strongest counterargument, and decision conditions. Keep technical evidence and source references in speaker notes where supported. Clearly distinguish observed findings from assumptions, estimates, and proposed actions. Do not bury decision-changing limitations in notes.

Judge and observable pass condition:
Run reproducible reconciliation and analysis checks using available existing tools; report their actual results. Where a held-out test is feasible, report its sample, baseline, and measured performance, including a miss. Have the confirmed existing finance or analytics reviewer check the definitions, scenario comparability, and whether the recommendation follows from the evidence, using those criteria explicitly.

Pass requires a deck of at most 12 slides ready for the meeting in two weeks, traceable quantitative claims, disclosed reconciliation differences and uncertainty, and a clear retention-versus-acquisition recommendation—or an explicit conditional decision when evidence is insufficient—with assumptions that would reverse it. Reviewer validation, reconciliation, or predictive validity remains unverified until the corresponding judge actually runs; never imply that unavailable evidence has been checked.
```

### Ledger

- `depth`: Use raw exports, reconciliation, cohort analysis, and reproducible checks rather than summary metrics; improves reliability. Depends on approved data access and an authoritative financial reference.
- `depth`: Test whether historical performance supports marginal investment returns; use later, unused cohorts when feasible. Reduces false confidence without requiring new experiments or tools.
- `range`: Apply decision science through comparable scenarios, break-even thresholds, sensitivity analysis, and opportunity cost; reveals when the recommendation changes. Adds analytical effort within existing capacity.
- `range`: Apply causal-inference checks and adversarial review to challenge attribution, selection effects, and intervention assumptions; exposes consequential decision risks without customer contact.
