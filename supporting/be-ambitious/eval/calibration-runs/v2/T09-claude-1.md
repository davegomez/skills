```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."

Deliverable: one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Method:
1. Break the original into its separate claims and label each one:
   - "was a mess": an evaluation of the work, stated as fact.
   - "everyone was confused": a claim about other people's internal states, which the manager may not be able to verify.
   - "Your": the subject the criticism attaches to.
   Mark which claim carries the required message (hard to follow) and which only add sting or overreach.
2. Apply two feedback methods. First, the observation-versus-evaluation distinction from Nonviolent Communication: replace verdicts with what the speaker actually experienced. Second, the impact part of Situation-Behavior-Impact: state the effect on the listener. Use only facts already present in the original sentence. Where a specific observed moment would strengthen the sentence, do not invent one. Instead, say in the explanation that the manager can add a real example they personally witnessed.
3. Apply face-threat analysis from politeness theory (Brown & Levinson). Identify what in the original threatens the colleague's standing, especially the global "mess" and the appeal to "everyone" as a crowd verdict. Remove that threat while keeping the criticism unmistakable. Candor fails if the message is softened until it can be missed.
4. Red-team the draft by reading it three ways:
   (a) As the colleague hearing it in private. Is it clear what was hard, and does it feel fair?
   (b) As the colleague hearing it with others present. Would it humiliate?
   (c) As a skeptic checking for euphemism. Would someone hearing it conclude the presentation was fine?
   Revise until it passes all three.

Output: exactly one revised sentence, then a short explanation (a few sentences) naming what changed and why. Do not add follow-up scripts, extra sentences, or process advice.

Judge, a rubric checked item by item, with a pass or fail and a one-line reason for each:
- Exactly one revised sentence.
- It states that the presentation was difficult to follow.
- Every factual claim in it is traceable to the original sentence or to the speaker's own experience. Nothing about the meeting is invented.
- No diagnosis of the colleague: no claims about their effort, skill, preparation, nerves, or character.
- No mention of performance management, documentation, or consequences.
- It passes red-team readings (a), (b), and (c).
- The explanation is short and names at least the "mess" change and the "everyone" change.
If the owner can show the original and the revision to a trusted reader, unlabeled, ask which one says the presentation was hard to follow and which one they would rather receive. Report that reader's answer as independent evidence. Until then, mark the rubric result as self-assessed.
Pass condition: every rubric item passes.
```
