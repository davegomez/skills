### Rewritten prompt

```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix. The intended readers and decision-makers are the team.

Requirements and boundaries:
- Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. Do not seek other evidence or attempt to reconstruct redacted information.
- Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy.
- Any move beyond producing the report must be a separate proposal requiring my approval. Do not execute it.

Assumption: the report should help the team prioritize fixes by supported customer impact and recurrence, rather than simply summarize incident volume. Label this assumption; do not invent business priorities or severity weights.

First checks:
- If the notes have not been supplied, request them before drafting.
- Check whether the notes establish the reporting week, incident identifiers, timestamps, impact, and resolution status. Ask for the reporting week if necessary; identify other missing evidence without seeking access elsewhere.
- Ask whether I can arrange a qualified team reviewer to check the report against the same redacted notes. Do not contact anyone. If independent review is unavailable, produce the report but label its validation pending.

Use these methods within the report’s scope:
1. Evidence matrix: assign stable reference labels to supplied notes and map each material claim to its supporting passages. Keep observed facts, reported diagnoses, your hypotheses, and unknowns distinct.
2. Failure-mode analysis: group incidents by observed checkout failure behavior. Treat a shared cause as established only when the notes support it. Check whether apparent recurrence reflects distinct incidents, duplicate reports, or one unresolved incident.
3. Decision analysis: prioritize candidate fixes using supported impact, distinct recurrence, persistence, and evidence confidence. Do not invent effort estimates. Separate recommendations to fix a supported problem from recommendations whose first step would be further investigation.
4. Sensitivity and red-team checks: test whether the priority order changes when ambiguous duplicates are merged or uncertain cause groupings are split. Look for evidence contradicting the leading diagnosis. Report unstable rankings instead of hiding them behind precise scores.

Produce a concise weekly report containing:
- Reporting window, source coverage, and a decision summary: what the team should consider fixing first, and why.
- A ranked failure-pattern table with note references, observed behavior, distinct incident counts where defensible, documented impact, cause confidence, resolution status, and recommended next decision.
- Material uncertainty, contradictory evidence, and limits on the ranking.
- A short evidence appendix sufficient to audit the counts and major claims.

Report measured results where the notes support them. Distinguish incident counts from failed attempts, affected customers, and failure rates. Do not calculate a checkout failure rate without a compatible numerator and denominator. Describe a change from last week only if the supplied notes support a comparable baseline. If the evidence cannot support a ranking, give a partial ordering or state what remains undecidable.

Keep any proposed work beyond the report in a clearly separate “Proposal — requires owner approval” section. Do not present proposed causes, fixes, or expected benefits as observed results.

Judge: provide the evidence appendix for a qualified independent team reviewer, arranged by me, to audit against the supplied redacted notes. The rubric is source fidelity, reconciled counts, defensible recurrence groupings, supported prioritization, explicit uncertainty, and compliance with every boundary above. Pass condition: the reviewer can trace every material factual claim to the notes, reproduce every reported tally, and finds no unsupported priority presented as settled or prohibited action taken. Report the reviewer’s documented findings; until that review occurs, label validation unverified.
```

- `depth` — Adds source-level traceability, failure-mode grouping, and duplicate reconciliation. Tests the premise that failures are genuinely recurring rather than repeated descriptions of one incident; requires only supplied notes.
- `depth` — Separates observed behavior, reported diagnoses, hypotheses, and missing denominators. Improves factual reliability without seeking production access or additional data sources.
