### Rewritten prompt

```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The required deliverable is a deck of at most 12 slides that helps leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition.

Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers. Do not make spending commitments or execute the investment decision. Added scope, new access, paid work, or irreversible actions require owner approval; do not assume that approval overrides these constraints.

Intended result and audience
Give leadership an evidence-backed investment recommendation, with its uncertainty, economic rationale, and the conditions under which the alternative would be better.

Assumptions to confirm: the decision concerns the next incremental investment, rather than replacing either function; sustainable economic return matters more than growth alone. Confirm the decision horizon, available investment envelope, and leadership’s success metric rather than silently choosing them.

First checks
The owner can provide raw customer and revenue exports but has not shared them yet. Request those exports through an approved channel, confirm permission to use them, and inspect their fields, coverage, definitions, and quality. Minimize customer-identifying information in analysis and slides.

Check whether existing tools and permitted records provide acquisition spend, retention costs, gross or contribution margins, attribution, customer tenure, and relevant historical interventions. Treat every unconfirmed source or capability as a dependency—not as available access. Prioritize analysis that fits the two-week deadline and existing staff capacity. Clearly identify what cannot be verified with available evidence.

Analysis
1. Reconstruct the quarter from raw records. Reconcile customer counts and revenue to available business totals; document definitions, exclusions, refunds, duplicates, missing values, and unexplained differences. Distinguish customer retention, revenue retention, and expansion.

2. Use cohort analysis to compare customers at equivalent tenure and segment by economically relevant characteristics supported by the data. Use survival analysis, if existing tools and data support it, to account for customers whose eventual retention is not yet observable. Avoid mistaking changes in customer mix for improved retention.

3. Test the premise that retention versus acquisition can be resolved from current headline metrics. Separate observed historical performance, predictive estimates, and causal claims. Do not treat all churn as preventable, attributed customers as incremental acquisitions, or historical averages as the return on new investment.

4. Use unit economics and decision analysis to compare the next investment in each option over the same horizon and on the same cost basis. Estimate incremental contribution and payback where evidence supports them. Model plausible ranges for acquisition incrementality, retention lift, costs, margins, and delivery capacity. Show the break-even thresholds at which the preferred option changes. If evidence cannot support causal return estimates, present conditional scenarios rather than invented precision.

5. Apply an adversarial review: build the strongest evidence-backed case against the initial recommendation. Test whether cohort mix, attribution, survivorship, or optimistic response assumptions reverse it. Where sufficient historical data exist, reserve a later period to check predictive estimates without tuning them on that period; report error and limitations. Predictive validation does not establish causal impact.

Deck
Stay within 12 slides; do not move essential reasoning into an oversized appendix. Lead with the decision, recommendation, and confidence. Include the quarter’s reconciled performance, cohort evidence, comparable investment economics, uncertainty and switching thresholds, the strongest counterargument, and the specific decision requested.

Distinguish observed findings from assumptions and proposals throughout. Cite source periods and definitions, and retain reproducible calculations in existing tools. If evidence is insufficient, explain the unresolved decision risk instead of forcing a confident recommendation. Any proposed additional investigation must respect the stated constraints and require approval if it adds scope.

Judge and observable pass condition
Use reproducible calculations against the supplied exports as the quantitative judge. Customer and revenue totals must reconcile to source records, with every discrepancy quantified and explained; the key analyses must rerun from documented inputs. Report measured reconciliation differences, any held-out prediction error, and the assumptions that change the recommendation.

Ask an existing qualified finance or analytics reviewer, if available within normal responsibilities, to assess source traceability, comparable economics, uncertainty, and whether the recommendation follows from the evidence. Report findings and unresolved objections; do not substitute self-assessment for independent review.

Pass condition: within two weeks, deliver a deck of no more than 12 slides that satisfies the operating constraints, supports each material quantitative claim with reproducible evidence or an explicitly labeled assumption, and gives leadership either a defensible investment choice with switching thresholds or a precise account of why the evidence cannot yet distinguish the options. Data-dependent findings and independent review remain explicitly unverified until their respective checks run.
```
