### Rewritten prompt

```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."

Give exactly one revised sentence and a short explanation. Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow. Do not invent what happened in the meeting, diagnose the colleague, or suggest a performance-management process.

Assumption: the manager wants wording they can use directly, not merely an analysis of why the original sounds harsh. Optimize for candid, usable feedback; do not claim that wording alone guarantees how the colleague will feel.

Use the original sentence as the only evidence:
- Apply close reading to distinguish the substantive feedback—difficulty following the presentation—from the disparaging label “a mess” and the unsupported claim about “everyone.”
- Apply a presupposition audit: check whether the revision introduces any unestablished cause, meeting detail, audience reaction, or claim about the colleague. Remove those additions.
- Use pragmatics’ distinction between criticizing work and threatening someone’s dignity: direct the criticism at the presentation’s comprehensibility, not the colleague’s competence or character. Do not dilute the feedback into vague encouragement.

Test the central premise that greater candor requires harsher language: make the difficulty explicit while removing insult and unsupported consensus. In the short explanation, identify how the revision achieves that balance without making claims about actual meeting reactions.

Stay within this wording task. Additional fact-finding, contacting the colleague, or any broader intervention requires owner approval.

First check whether owner-provided feedback from a manager or workplace-communication reviewer is available. Do not assume reviewer access or contact anyone. If none is available, still provide the requested sentence and explanation, but identify independent validation as unverified in the explanation.

Judge: a manager or qualified workplace-communication reviewer evaluates the wording against this rubric. Pass only if it clearly conveys that the presentation was difficult to follow, criticizes the work without disparaging the person, makes no unsupported factual additions or diagnoses, proposes no performance-management process, and contains exactly one revised sentence plus a short explanation. Report documented reviewer findings if available; otherwise leave this judgment explicitly unverified.
```
