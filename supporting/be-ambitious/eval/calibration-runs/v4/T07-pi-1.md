### Rewritten prompt

```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix.

Assumption: the report should support a defensible fix order, not merely summarize incidents. Improve the decision support within the report; do not undertake fixes or establish a new recurring workflow.

Boundaries:
- Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide.
- Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy.
- Do not seek unredacted records or reconstruct redacted identities.
- Treat recommendations as recommendations, not authorization to act.

First check whether the notes and reporting period are supplied, and whether an independent reviewer has been designated for the check below. If the period is unclear, ask rather than assume. If notes are missing, request them rather than invent findings. Do not contact a reviewer or obtain additional evidence yourself.

Build the report from a traceable incident-to-failure-pattern evidence matrix:
- Reference each supplied note using its existing identifier or an assigned neutral label.
- Separate distinct incidents from multiple descriptions of the same incident. Flag ambiguous duplicates rather than silently merging them.
- Distinguish observed symptoms, reported impact, suspected mechanisms, and confirmed causes. Shared symptoms alone do not establish a shared cause.
- Count recurrence only within the supplied notes. Do not infer checkout-wide failure rates, trends, or revenue loss without supporting denominators or measurements.
- Treat the notes as a potentially incomplete incident sample, not a representative record of all checkout attempts.

Use these methods to make the report decision-ready:
1. Reliability engineering: group recurring failure modes by checkout stage and observed behavior, while preserving evidence that apparently similar incidents may have different causes.
2. Decision analysis: compare candidate fix priorities using documented recurrence, customer impact, strength of evidence, and any documented implementation constraints. Mark unknown effort or impact as unknown; avoid unsupported numerical scores.
3. Adversarial hypothesis testing: for each leading priority, identify the strongest alternative explanation or counterexample in the notes. Check whether the priority changes under plausible interpretations of ambiguous incidents or missing impact data.

Deliver a concise weekly report containing:
- An executive summary of what the notes support.
- A table of recurring patterns, distinct-incident counts, documented impact, evidence references, suspected versus confirmed causes, and important gaps.
- A proposed fix order, with the rationale and uncertainty for each priority. Distinguish “best-supported fix candidate” from “needs investigation before choosing a fix.” If the evidence cannot distinguish priorities, say so.
- A short section explaining what would change the recommendation.
- A validation-status line.

With my approval only: undertake any work beyond this report. Present such work as a separate proposal identifying its purpose, scope, evidence requirements, risks, and requested authorization; do not execute it.
With my approval only: obtain an independent review from an owner-designated checkout or incident specialist using only the same redacted notes. Do not contact anyone or share materials yourself.

Judge: the approved independent reviewer checks the report against the supplied notes. Pass requires every factual finding and count to be traceable, no unsupported cause or population-wide rate claims, and each priority to have an evidence-based rationale with material uncertainty and counterevidence disclosed. The reviewer must be able to reconstruct the proposed ordering—or agree that the evidence does not support one. Until that review occurs, label the report “independently unverified”; do not claim it passed.
```
