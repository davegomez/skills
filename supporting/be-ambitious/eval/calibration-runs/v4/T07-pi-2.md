Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix.

Assumed goal: help the team choose the most defensible next fix, rather than simply summarize incidents. Recommendations are not authorization to implement them.

Boundaries:
- Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide.
- Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy.
- Do not reconstruct redacted information or supplement the notes with outside sources.
- If you recommend moving beyond the report, present that as a separate proposal requiring my approval.

First check whether the notes and reporting week have been supplied. Identify missing inputs without seeking them elsewhere. If notes are absent, request them rather than inventing a report. If dates, counts, or coverage are incomplete, state what cannot be established.

Use these methods within the report:
1. Evidence reconstruction: build a compact incident timeline from the notes, assigning stable references if needed. Separate distinct incidents from duplicate descriptions. Distinguish observed symptoms, reported impact, suspected causes, and confirmed causes; preserve uncertainty and conflicting accounts.
2. Reliability analysis: group recurring failure modes by supported similarities, not merely similar wording. Test the premise that repeated symptoms share a cause by looking for differences and counterexamples in the supplied notes. Label causal explanations as hypotheses unless the notes substantiate them.
3. Decision analysis: prioritize candidate fixes using documented impact, recurrence, and confidence that the proposed intervention addresses the failure. Include effort or feasibility only when supported by the notes. Do not invent scores, conversion losses, exposure denominators, or engineering estimates. Distinguish counts in these notes from production failure rates.
4. Sensitivity analysis: ask whether reasonable alternative interpretations of ambiguous incidents would change the recommended priority. If the ranking is unstable, show the competing choices and the missing evidence that would distinguish them rather than forcing a winner.
5. Red-team the leading recommendation: identify the strongest evidence against it, a plausible alternative explanation, and the conditions under which a different fix should take priority.

Keep the weekly report concise and decision-ready:
- Executive summary: leading recommendation, supporting evidence, and principal uncertainty.
- Recurring failure modes: incident references, documented impact and recurrence, causal confidence, and changes over time only where the supplied notes support comparison.
- Prioritized fix candidates: rationale, counterevidence, and ranking sensitivity.
- Decision requested: what the team can reasonably choose now, or why the evidence does not yet support a defensible choice.
- Limitations: missing coverage and unresolved questions. Do not represent a proposed fix as validated.

With my approval only: arrange a qualified independent team review using only the report and supplied redacted notes. Present this as a separate proposal; do not contact anyone or share materials yourself.

Judge: the independent reviewer checks traceability, incident deduplication, separation of observation from hypothesis, and whether the recommended priority survives the stated alternative interpretations. Pass when every material factual claim traces to the notes, no unsupported causal or quantitative claim remains, and the recommendation is either defensible under those alternatives or explicitly conditional on named missing evidence. First confirm reviewer approval and availability; until the review runs, label the report’s decision quality unverified.
