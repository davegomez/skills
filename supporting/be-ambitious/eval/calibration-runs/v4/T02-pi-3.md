```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck is required and must contain at most 12 slides. Its purpose is to help leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. Use existing staff and tools only. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers.

Assumption to confirm: leaders want the investment that creates the greatest incremental economic value within their budget, capacity, and acceptable payback period—not simply the highest retention rate or customer count.

First check dependencies:
- Request the raw customer and revenue exports; they are available from the owner but have not yet been shared. Confirm their coverage, grain, identifiers, timestamps, and revenue definitions before drawing conclusions.
- Ask for the decision horizon, investment constraints, and any already-available acquisition costs, retention costs, margins, and historical intervention results. Treat these as dependencies, not facts.
- Confirm which existing tools can perform the analysis and which existing leadership participant can independently review its qualitative claims during the normal deck-review or leadership process. Do not create a new meeting or assign additional staff without authorization.
- If dependencies remain missing, still prepare the required deck within two weeks. Mark unsupported conclusions as unverified and identify precisely what evidence prevents a defensible choice.

Build a decision-ready argument, not merely a retrospective:
1. State the retention-versus-acquisition decision and the evidence-backed recommendation, or explain why the available evidence does not support choosing yet.
2. Establish the quarterly baseline from raw records. Reconcile reported revenue to the exports; disclose duplicates, missing identifiers, incomplete periods, and unresolved differences.
3. Use cohort analysis to distinguish customer tenure and acquisition vintage from genuine changes in retention or customer value. Separate customer retention from revenue retention, and distinguish new-customer revenue, expansion, contraction, and churn where the records support those categories. Do not invent business-model-specific metrics.
4. Compare retention and acquisition on a common investment amount and time horizon, using incremental contribution, cash requirements, and payback where inputs permit. Distinguish historical accounting measures from forward-looking estimates, and avoid double-counting revenue.
5. Test the belief underlying the choice: does the apparent advantage survive cohort adjustment, realistic intervention costs, and feasible improvements? Use existing historical experiments or credible comparison groups if available. Otherwise label estimated intervention effects as assumptions; descriptive churn or acquisition correlations do not prove investment returns.
6. Apply decision analysis: show a small set of evidence-grounded scenarios and the break-even assumptions at which the preferred investment changes. Identify the uncertainty most capable of reversing the recommendation. Do not use invented probabilities or false precision.
7. Red-team the recommendation by making the strongest evidence-based case for the other investment. Check whether selection bias, incomplete customer histories, or shared cost allocation explains the apparent winner.
8. End with the specific decision leaders should make, its constraints, and any evidence gap that would justify deferral. Any proposed follow-up remains a proposal, not authorization to execute.

Keep all findings, source references, material assumptions, limitations, and validation status within the required deck. Use no more than 12 slides; prioritize a clear decision narrative over exhaustive analysis. Do not add a separate required report, new operating workflow, customer outreach, or paid tooling.

Judge: Use reproducible checks in existing tools for mechanical claims, and an existing leadership participant who did not author the analysis for qualitative review. Confirm reviewer availability first; if unavailable, leave qualitative validation unverified. Pass requires: at most 12 slides, ready for the meeting in two weeks; headline historical figures traceable to supplied records with reconciliations passing or discrepancies explicitly disclosed; retention and acquisition compared on stated common terms; intervention assumptions separated from observed evidence; and the reviewer able to identify the recommendation or justified deferral, its economic basis, and the assumptions that would reverse it. Report actual check results in the deck. Until each check runs, leave its affected claims unverified.
```

**Ledger**
- `altitude`: Moves from a quarterly review supporting a choice to an explicit, economically comparable investment decision, including reversal conditions; preserves the deck, deadline, and operating boundaries.
- `depth`: Adds raw-record reconciliation and cohort analysis to distinguish genuine performance changes from data errors and customer-mix effects; depends on exports and existing analytical tools.
- `depth`: Tests whether observed performance supports incremental investment returns using available historical intervention evidence; missing causal evidence remains an explicit limitation, not a fabricated finding.
- `range`: Borrows managerial accounting to compare contribution, cash requirements, and payback without double-counting; depends on existing cost and margin information.
- `range`: Adds decision-analysis break-even scenarios and an adversarial case for the alternative investment; exposes decision fragility without launching experiments or changing cadence.
- `depth`: Adds reproducible mechanical checks and independent qualitative review within the existing leadership process; reviewer availability is unconfirmed, and no new staffing is authorized.
- Skipped lenses: None; all three contribute distinct, in-scope improvements.
- Unresolved owner actions: Supply exports; confirm the economic objective, horizon, budget, and capacity constraints; identify available cost and intervention evidence, existing tools, and an existing leadership reviewer.
