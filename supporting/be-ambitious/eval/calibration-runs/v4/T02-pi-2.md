```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. Leaders must use it to decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. The deck is required and must contain at most 12 slides. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers. Recommend an investment decision; do not implement it.

Assumption to confirm: leadership wants to improve durable, economically valuable growth, rather than maximize customer count or revenue regardless of cost.

First check the dependencies:
- Request the raw customer and revenue exports, which have been offered but not yet shared. Confirm their coverage, grain, definitions, and permitted use.
- Confirm which existing tools are available and whether already-authorized records include acquisition spending, gross margins, and retention-program costs. Do not assume revenue exports establish profitability or intervention effectiveness.
- Confirm the investment amount or constraint, decision horizon, and leadership’s success criterion. If unavailable, label assumptions and show how the recommendation changes across plausible values.
- Confirm whether an appropriately qualified leader can assess the recommendation at the already-planned meeting. Missing data, tool access, or review capacity must remain explicit dependencies; do not invent findings.

Build a decision instrument, not merely a retrospective:
- Show what changed this quarter, which mechanisms explain it, and what those mechanisms imply for the retention-versus-acquisition decision.
- Compare the two options over the same horizon and investment basis. Show their estimated incremental contribution, timing, uncertainty, and break-even conditions where the available evidence supports those quantities.
- Recommend one option when justified. Otherwise make the choice conditional on explicit thresholds and identify exactly what remains unknown. Separate a proposed investment from any authority to execute it.

Use professional analytical methods:
- Reconcile export totals to available authoritative business totals. Check duplicates, missing identifiers, date coverage, currency, and metric definitions; disclose material discrepancies.
- Build a revenue bridge separating new customers, churn, contraction, expansion, and other supported effects. Use it to test the premise that retention or acquisition is the actual growth constraint, rather than assuming either diagnosis.
- Analyze customer and revenue retention by cohort and tenure. Compare cohorts at equivalent ages so newer cohorts are not mistaken for better performers merely because they have had less time to churn.
- Distinguish observed performance from causal intervention effects. Historical churn is not automatically preventable churn; historical acquisition economics do not automatically describe the next investment.

Borrow methods from decision science and adversarial analysis:
- Use sensitivity analysis to identify the assumptions that reverse the preferred investment. Show the switching thresholds, not just a favorable point estimate.
- Red-team the recommendation by constructing the strongest evidence-supported case for the other option. Identify the consequential claim most vulnerable to being wrong, and whether available evidence actually resolves it.
- Treat scenarios as scenarios, not forecasts validated by observation.

Deliver only the deck, with evidence provenance, material assumptions, limitations, and validation status inside its 12-slide limit. A useful structure is: decision and recommendation; quarterly performance; revenue bridge; cohort evidence; acquisition economics; retention economics; comparable investment cases; uncertainty and switching thresholds; counterargument; decision requested. Combine or omit sections as needed without hiding material limitations.

Judge and observable pass condition: before finalizing, use available existing tools to verify the slide count, reconciliation checks, and model arithmetic. These mechanical checks pass only if the deck has at most 12 slides and material discrepancies are resolved or prominently bounded. At the scheduled meeting, a qualified leader independent of the deck’s author should assess whether both options are compared on a common basis, claims are traceable to evidence, causal uncertainty is explicit, and the requested decision and switching thresholds are clear. Record check results and any documented review finding within the deck; until a check or review occurs, label that aspect unverified. An evidence-insufficient but explicit conditional decision is acceptable; an unsupported confident recommendation is not.
```
