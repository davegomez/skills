### Rewritten prompt

```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix. Assumption: the report should help reduce repeat failures by distinguishing well-supported fix candidates from symptoms that still need investigation. Recommend decisions; do not implement them.

You may inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy. Do not seek external evidence or attempt to reconstruct redacted information.

First check whether the supplied notes establish the reporting week, incident dates, and enough detail to distinguish separate incidents from duplicate accounts. Ask me for missing notes or reporting boundaries rather than inventing them. Also check whether I have identified an already-authorized reviewer familiar with checkout incidents; do not contact or recruit anyone.

Make the report decision-ready:
- Give a concise executive summary and a ranked shortlist of fix candidates, with the evidence, expected benefit, uncertainty, and decision needed for each. If the evidence cannot support a ranking, explain why.
- Separate “supported fix candidate” from “needs investigation.” A recommendation is not authorization to investigate or implement.
- Include a compact evidence table and limitations section so readers can audit the recommendations.

Use incident-level evidence rather than simply summarizing the notes:
- Assign local source references without reproducing unnecessary sensitive detail. Extract the documented timing, checkout stage, symptom, impact, suspected cause, and any recorded intervention or outcome. Mark missing fields as unknown.
- Deduplicate repeated descriptions of the same incident. Distinguish incident counts from mentions; distinguish counts within these notes from production-wide failure rates.
- Use reliability engineering’s fault-tree reasoning to distinguish observed symptoms, possible failure mechanisms, and evidenced causes. Label hypotheses explicitly and cite the notes supporting or contradicting them.
- Test the central assumption that recurring symptoms represent a recurring underlying defect. Look for counterexamples, different mechanisms producing similar symptoms, and cases inconsistent with the leading explanation. Do not turn a plausible grouping into a confirmed root cause.

Use decision analysis to make prioritization robust:
- Compare candidates using documented recurrence, documented impact, confidence in the explanation, and evidence that the proposed fix would address it. Include effort or implementation risk only if the notes support them.
- Avoid invented numerical scores or unsupported precision. Explain the trade-offs behind the ordering.
- Stress-test the top recommendation by changing uncertain groupings or assumptions within what the notes permit. Say whether the preferred choice holds or changes, and identify which missing fact could change the decision.

Keep all findings bounded to the supplied notes. Distinguish observed evidence, inference, and proposed action throughout. If coverage is incomplete, say what cannot be concluded; do not imply that unmentioned failures did not occur.

With my approval only: pursue any activity beyond this report. If you recommend one, describe it as a separate proposal specifying its purpose, evidence needed, permissions, and risks. Do not execute it, and do not treat approval of the report as permission to lift any prohibition above.

Judge: an already-authorized, owner-designated reviewer familiar with checkout incidents checks the report against the supplied notes. Pass only if every material factual claim and ranking rationale is traceable, duplicates are handled explicitly, causes are not overstated, uncertainties and ranking sensitivity are visible, and no prohibited activity occurred. The report must let the reviewer identify the leading fix candidate—or understand why the evidence cannot support one. Until that review occurs, label the report’s validation status “unverified.”
```
