### Prompt

```text
Tell me why customers churned last month. I have no raw event logs, CRM export, or database access to share right now. All I know is that the sales team thinks onboarding is the cause. Do not buy data, scrape customer information, or request production credentials.

Assumption to make explicit: the explanation is intended to help decide whether onboarding deserves priority. Improve that decision without pretending the available information establishes a cause.

First check what evidence is actually supplied and whether an owner-designated independent reviewer is already available and authorized. Do not assume access to tools, customer records, or reviewers. Missing evidence must not block a useful, bounded response; missing review must leave the assessment unreviewed.

Produce a concise diagnostic brief:

1. What we can conclude.
   State directly that the cause of last month's churn cannot be determined from the supplied information. Treat the sales team's view as a reported hypothesis—not a verified finding or independent corroboration. Do not invent churn rates, customer segments, timelines, quotations, or confidence percentages. Note that the churn definition and affected population are unknown.

2. Test the onboarding explanation rather than repeat it.
   Use structured competing-hypothesis analysis: compare onboarding with up to three plausible alternative explanations, explicitly labeled as possibilities rather than customer facts. For each, describe:
   - The mechanism by which it could produce churn.
   - An observation that would distinguish it from the alternatives.
   - Evidence that would weaken it.

   Use causal reasoning to distinguish an onboarding problem that precedes and contributes to churn from an onboarding complaint that merely accompanies another problem. Include the possibility that the sales team's observations are not representative of all customers who churned.

3. Identify the smallest useful evidence check.
   Describe the rawest evidence needed to discriminate between the leading possibilities, rather than relying on another summarized opinion. Treat every unavailable source as a future dependency, not something you can access now. Explain what comparison would be informative and what it would still fail to establish. Evidence used to develop a revised explanation cannot also count as independent confirmation; require fresh evidence for confirmation or leave the explanation unverified.

4. Give a decision boundary.
   Explain whether the present evidence justifies prioritizing an onboarding fix, and separate that from whether a limited investigation is worth considering. Apply value-of-information reasoning: identify the evidence check most likely to change the decision with the least burden, without fabricating numerical costs or probabilities. Recommend only; do not execute changes or collect data.

With my approval only: collect additional evidence through interviews, outreach, or newly shared records; agree the scope and privacy boundaries first. The prohibitions on buying data, scraping customer information, and requesting production credentials still apply.
With my approval only: involve an independent reviewer or share this brief with anyone not already authorized.

Judge: an already-authorized or subsequently approved independent reviewer checks the brief against the supplied information. Pass only if every target-specific assertion is traceable to that information or explicitly labeled as an assumption, hypothesis, or unknown; the hypotheses have distinguishable tests and disconfirming evidence; the recommendation does not outrun the evidence; and all access and collection restrictions are preserved. Report review status. Without that review, leave the brief unreviewed. Passing this review validates the diagnostic reasoning—not the cause of churn, which remains unverified without supporting evidence.
```

### Ledger

- `altitude`: From an unsupported explanation of last month’s churn to a bounded explanation that helps decide whether onboarding deserves priority; adds a recommendation, not authority to act.
- `depth`: Replaces acceptance of the sales theory with competing hypotheses, disconfirmation, and raw-evidence requirements; no unavailable access assumed.
- `depth`: Separates evidence used to develop an explanation from fresh confirmation; prevents a revised hypothesis from being presented as a finding.
- `range`: Causal reasoning exposes reverse causation and unrepresentative observations; value-of-information reasoning identifies a useful next check without invented estimates.
- `depth`: Adds an independent-review rubric matched to the limited evidence; review is conditional on access and authorization, and cannot validate the actual churn cause.
- Skipped lenses: None; all three contribute within the diagnostic scope.
- Owner actions: None needed for the bounded brief. Approve any additional collection or reviewer involvement before it happens; causal attribution remains unresolved without evidence.
