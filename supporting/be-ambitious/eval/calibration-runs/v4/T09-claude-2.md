```text
Critique this sentence for a manager who wants to be candid without humiliating a colleague: "Your presentation was a mess and everyone was confused."

Goal (assumption: the manager wants the colleague to hear the problem clearly, keep their dignity, and leave able to act on it).

Method:
1. Critique the original phrase by phrase:
   - "a mess" is a global judgment with nothing to act on.
   - "everyone was confused" is a universal claim the manager may not be able to verify, and it implies a group verdict.
   - Check what the sentence leaves out that the colleague would need in order to respond.
2. Apply named feedback methods and state what each changes:
   - Situation-Behavior-Impact: tie the point to the presentation and its effect on the listener, not to the person.
   - Nonviolent Communication's split between observation and evaluation: replace labels like "mess" with what can be stated as experienced.
   - Brown and Levinson's face-threat analysis: say the point plainly, on record, without adding avoidable threat to the colleague's standing.
   - Speak from the manager's own experience ("I found...") instead of reporting other people's reactions.
3. Red-team the draft from both sides:
   - Read it as the colleague on a bad day. Does any word still read as contempt or exposure?
   - Read it as a skeptic of euphemism. Is the message "the presentation was hard to follow" still unmistakable, or has it been softened away?
   - Revise until both readings pass.

Requirements:
- Give exactly one revised sentence and a short explanation.
- Preserve the message that the presentation was difficult to follow.
- Do not invent what happened in the meeting.
- Do not diagnose the colleague.
- Do not suggest a performance-management process.
- The explanation may say the manager can add a specific moment they observed themselves, but must not supply one.

Judge: a fresh reviewer (a separate model instance or a person) who has not seen the explanation scores the revised sentence against this rubric. Each item is pass or fail:
(a) the difficulty following the presentation is stated unmistakably;
(b) it contains no global label for the work or the person;
(c) it makes no claim about other attendees' reactions;
(d) it invents no meeting details;
(e) it makes no diagnosis of the colleague;
(f) it mentions no performance process;
(g) it is a single sentence.
Pass condition: all seven items pass. Report the scores with the deliverable. Until the reviewer has run, the sentence is unverified.
```
