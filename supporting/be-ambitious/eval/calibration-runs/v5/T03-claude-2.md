```text
Write a quiet free-verse poem about the smell of rain on warm pavement. It is for its own sake, not for marketing or publication.

Requirements, all of which hold:
- Plain language.
- Under 120 words.
- Free verse, with no rhyme scheme.
- Output only the poem. No preface, and no explanatory note after the poem.

How to write it:
- Start from the raw sensation, not the word for it. Before drafting, privately list concrete particulars of the moment. Examples: the first drops darkening dry concrete in coin-sized spots, heat lifting off the ground, the smell arriving before the sound, dust and hot stone, and what a person does when the smell reaches them. Draft from that list.
- Do not name the phenomenon ("petrichor"). Avoid stock descriptors such as "earthy" or "fresh" unless you make them specific.
- Borrow the perfumer's habit of describing a scent over time: what hits first, what lingers, what remains at the end. Let the poem move the way the smell does, from arriving to deepening to fading as the pavement cools. Do not use any perfumery terms.
- Keep it quiet. Use short lines and soft stresses, with no exclamations. End on an image, not a statement or moral.
- Revise like a working poet. Write at least two distinct drafts from your list and keep the stronger one. Sound it out line by line and cut every word that carries neither an image nor the poem's movement. Replace each abstraction with a concrete noun or verb.

Judge, applied silently before you return the poem:
- Count the words, including any title. The count must be under 120.
- Scan the line endings for end-rhyme pairs or a repeating sound pattern, and remove any you find.
- Scan for any word a general reader would need to look up, and replace it.
- Pass when all three checks hold and the output contains nothing but the poem.
- Whether the poem is quiet and true to the smell remains unverified until the owner reads it.
```

- `depth`: The prompt drafts from a private list of concrete sensory details instead of the generic idea of "rain smell," bans clichés and the jargon name, and requires two drafts plus a revision pass that cuts by sound. This gives sharper, less stock imagery with no change to scope or length.
- `depth`: Word count, rhyme, and plain-language checks run silently, so the output stays poem-only as you required.
- `range`: Perfumery's way of describing a scent over time (first impression, what lingers, what remains) gives the poem a natural shape from arrival to fade, without importing any of its vocabulary.
- Skipped: `altitude`. You want the poem for its own sake, so there is no downstream decision or action to climb toward.
- Owner actions: None are required. You are the judge of whether it is quiet and rings true, so read it aloud when it comes back.
