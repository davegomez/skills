## Rewritten prompt

```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck is required, must be at most 12 slides including any appendix, and must help leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers.

Assumption: the investment decision should favor incremental economic value, not simply whichever activity produces the larger historical revenue figure.

First check dependencies: request the raw customer and revenue exports, which have not yet been shared; confirm the review quarter, available historical coverage, field definitions, and which existing tools are available and authorized. Ask whether an investment amount, decision horizon, and relevant cost or intervention-effect evidence are already available for this review. Do not assume access to additional systems or data. Identify missing inputs and proceed only as far as the available evidence supports.

Make this a decision deck, not a reporting deck:
- Open with the recommended choice—or explicitly explain why the evidence cannot yet distinguish retention from acquisition.
- Show the economic assumptions and break-even conditions under which the choice would reverse.
- Close with the specific decision leaders need to make, the main uncertainty, and the consequences of choosing incorrectly. Do not implement the decision or establish a new workflow.

Build the argument from the raw exports:
- Use a revenue bridge to separate new-customer revenue, retained-customer revenue, expansion, contraction, and churn where the available fields support those categories. State definitions and reconcile the bridge to export totals without double-counting.
- Use customer-cohort analysis to compare retention and revenue at equal customer ages. Show denominators and observation windows so recent, incomplete cohorts do not look artificially worse.
- Test the premise that the largest historical revenue loss or growth source is also the best investment opportunity. Distinguish the size of an opportunity from the portion an investment could actually change.
- Use contribution economics when supported by available cost data. If only revenue is available, label revenue as a proxy and do not present it as profit or investment return.

Borrow two methods from adjacent fields:
- Decision analysis: compare retention and acquisition under the same investment amount and time horizon, using sensitivity analysis and break-even thresholds. Separate observed inputs from scenario assumptions; show which uncertain inputs could reverse the recommendation. If no investment amount is supplied, use clearly labeled comparable scenarios rather than inventing an approved budget.
- Causal inference: audit whether apparent advantages could reflect customer mix, cohort maturity, seasonality, or selection rather than an intervention’s effect. Historical association alone does not establish incremental return. Red-team the preferred option with the strongest plausible case for the alternative and show whether the choice survives.

Keep the deck concise. Put source references, definitions, reconciliation results, limitations, and essential calculation details in slide notes where appropriate. Report measured findings only after checking them. If evidence is missing, still prepare the required deck, but label affected findings and recommendations provisional or unverified; do not fill gaps with invented results.

With my approval only: ask an existing, qualified finance or analytics colleague to independently review the calculations and decision argument, using existing tools and without sharing data beyond already-authorized access.

Judge: first verify that the existing tools can perform the numerical checks and that any independent review is approved and available. Mechanical passage requires at most 12 slides, reconciled totals with any rounding differences documented, reproducible key figures, consistent comparison horizons, and a clear distinction between observed evidence and assumptions. Decision-quality passage requires the approved independent reviewer to confirm that the recommendation follows from the evidence, the strongest alternative is fairly represented, and material recommendation-reversing thresholds are visible. Record check results and review status in the deck’s notes. Missing data or an unavailable reviewer leaves the affected claims or decision-quality assessment unverified, not passed.
```
