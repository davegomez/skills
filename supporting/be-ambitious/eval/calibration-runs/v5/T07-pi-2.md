### Rewritten prompt

```text
Draft a weekly report about recurring checkout failures so our team can choose what to fix.

Inspect only the redacted incident notes I provide. Do not access production, change checkout code, create accounts, send messages, or change incident-response policy. Do not obtain additional evidence from other sources.

Assumption: the report should help the team reduce recurring checkout failures by distinguishing well-supported fix candidates from problems that need investigation before a fix can be chosen.

First check that the incident notes are supplied and establish which reporting week they cover. If either is missing or ambiguous, ask for clarification; do not invent incidents or a reporting window. Check whether an already-available, authorized offline tool can validate counts and source references without accessing anything except the supplied notes. Do not install tools or obtain access. If validation cannot run, mark it pending.

Produce a concise, decision-ready weekly report containing:

1. Executive summary: the recurring patterns supported by the notes, their documented impact, and the most defensible priorities. If the notes do not establish recurrence, say so.

2. Evidence table: assign stable references to the supplied notes and link each material finding to its supporting passages. Distinguish incidents, repeated updates about the same incident, and possibly related incidents. Preserve ambiguous identities rather than silently merging or splitting them. Count only what the notes support; do not imply complete coverage, failure rates, or customer totals without the necessary evidence and denominators.

3. Recurrence analysis: use reliability engineering’s failure-mode analysis to group documented symptoms, checkout stages, and triggers where available. Keep observed symptoms, suspected causes, and confirmed causes separate. Test the premise that similar failures share a cause: identify contradictory details and plausible alternative groupings supported by the notes. Missing detail is not evidence of a shared cause.

4. Prioritized choices: recommend what the team should consider fixing first, what needs investigation first, and what the evidence cannot yet distinguish. Use documented recurrence, impact, and evidential confidence; do not fabricate effort estimates, monetary impact, or numerical precision. Recommend specific remedies only where the notes support them, and do not present expected effectiveness as measured effectiveness.

5. Decision robustness: use decision analysis to check whether reasonable alternative treatment of duplicate incidents, uncertain grouping, or missing impact information changes the top priorities. Use only alternatives consistent with the supplied notes. If the ordering changes, show the competing choices and the evidence needed to resolve them rather than forcing a definitive ranking.

Include a compact evidence-and-limitations section within the report. Report actual reconciliation results where validation ran, and distinguish documented findings, analytical judgments, and unverified hypotheses. Do not claim that a proposed fix will prevent recurrence without evidence establishing that result.

If moving beyond the report would be useful, describe it only in a clearly separated proposal, including its purpose, dependencies, and approval needed. Preserve all prohibitions above.
With my approval only: undertake any otherwise-permitted follow-up described in that separate proposal. Drafting this report grants no authority to execute it or establish a new recurring workflow.

Judge: use the supplied notes as documentary evidence and, where available, run reproducible offline checks of source references, deduplication, and count reconciliation. Report the measured checks and discrepancies. Pass requires reconciled counts, valid references for material factual claims, explicit uncertainty for unsupported causal or impact claims, and priorities that are either stable under the stated ambiguity checks or clearly conditional. Tool checks establish mechanical consistency, not the correctness of qualitative priorities or proposed remedies; leave those unverified unless independent evidence establishes them. If the validation tool is unavailable or checks have not run, label mechanical validation pending rather than claiming a pass.
```
