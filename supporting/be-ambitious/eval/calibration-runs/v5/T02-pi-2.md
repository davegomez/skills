## Rewritten prompt

```text
Prepare the quarterly business review deck for our leadership meeting in two weeks. The deck is required, must be at most 12 slides, and must help leaders decide whether to invest in retention or acquisition. Use existing staff and tools. Do not change our operating cadence or contact customers.

Assumption: the deck should support a defensible investment decision, not merely describe last quarter’s performance.
Assumption: comparable incremental economic value is a useful decision criterion; confirm leadership’s objective, investment horizon, budget constraint, and risk tolerance before treating it as the governing criterion.

First check dependencies:
- Request the raw customer and revenue exports, which have not yet been shared. Confirm their coverage, definitions, permitted use, and whether customers can be linked consistently across periods.
- Check which existing tools can analyze the exports, produce the deck, and validate its slide count and calculations.
- Identify whether acquisition spend, retention costs, margins, and any historical intervention evidence are already available within authorized sources. Do not assume access or invent missing inputs.
- Check whether qualified independent review is available using existing staff and tools, subject to the approval below.

Build a decision-ready QBR within the 12-slide limit:
1. Establish quarterly performance from the raw exports. Reconcile revenue totals, check duplicate customers and missing records, and distinguish new, retained, reactivated, and lost customers using explicit definitions.
2. Use cohort analysis to compare customers at similar tenure, and a revenue bridge to distinguish acquisition, retention, expansion, and contraction where the data supports those distinctions. Show what changed and why it matters to the investment choice.
3. Compare retention and acquisition on the same investment basis and time horizon. Separate observed results from assumptions and modeled scenarios. If costs or margins are missing, show the decision’s dependence on them rather than presenting revenue as profit.
4. Test the premise that the available evidence can establish which investment has greater incremental value. Historical differences and predictive correlations alone do not establish the effect of additional spending. Use any suitable existing intervention evidence; otherwise label causal claims unverified.
5. Apply decision analysis: identify the break-even assumptions under which the preferred option changes, including uncertainty about retention lift, acquisition efficiency, and payback where relevant. Recommend an option only as strongly as the evidence permits. If the choice remains unresolved, name the specific missing evidence that prevents it.

Use a red-team counterargument to test the strongest case against the recommendation. Check whether tenure differences, customer mix, attribution, or timing could reverse the apparent advantage. If this changes an empirical claim, confirm it on a comparable period or customer subset not used to develop the revision when enough data exists; otherwise leave it unverified.

Keep the main narrative concise: quarterly results, economic drivers, comparable investment options, uncertainty, and the decision requested. Put source references, definitions, material assumptions, and validation findings within the same deck; do not rely on extra slides or a separate deliverable. Do not invent findings to fill gaps.

With my approval only: ask a qualified existing staff member who did not produce the analysis to independently review the recommendation and its evidence, within the existing cadence and before the meeting if feasible.

Judge: use available existing tools to verify at most 12 slides, reproduce the material calculations from the supplied exports, and reconcile totals or document discrepancies in the deck. For decision quality, the approved independent reviewer should check that both options use comparable economics, uncertainty is visible, evidence is traceable, and the recommendation survives the strongest supported counterargument—or explicitly states why no choice is yet justified. Pass means the required deck is ready for the meeting in two weeks, satisfies the mechanical checks, and passes that review. Until the reviewer runs, qualitative decision quality remains unverified; missing data must remain visibly unresolved.
```
